01

Independence in two languages

In statistics: frequencies

Observe two variables on the same \(n\) units and count the pairs: \(n_{ij}\) units have \(X=x_i\) and \(Y=y_j\). Row and column totals are the margins \(n_{i\cdot}=\sum_j n_{ij}\), \(n_{\cdot j}=\sum_i n_{ij}\).

\(X\) and \(Y\) are independent in the data when every conditional distribution of \(Y\), one per value of \(X\), has the same shape as the overall distribution of \(Y\):

$$\frac{n_{ij}}{n_{i\cdot}}=\frac{n_{\cdot j}}{n}\ \ \forall i,j \iff f_{ij}=f_{i\cdot}\,f_{\cdot j} \iff n_{ij}=\hat n_{ij}=\frac{n_{i\cdot}\,n_{\cdot j}}{n}.$$

Knowing the row tells you nothing new about the column. Real data never hit this ideal exactly, so independence becomes a question of distance from it.

In probability: a definition

In Kolmogorov's space \((\Omega,\mathcal F,P)\), \(X\) and \(Y\) are random variables (measurable maps). They are independent when, for every pair of values,

$$P(X=x_i,\,Y=y_j)=P(X=x_i)\,P(Y=y_j),$$

equivalently \(P(Y=y_j\mid X=x_i)=P(Y=y_j)\), since \(P(B\mid A)=P(A\cap B)/P(A)\) by definition. The second form is the intuitive one (conditioning does not change the distribution), the product is its algebraic form. The frequency identity on the left is exactly what motivates this definition: in probability it is not a theorem, it is the rule.

Measuring the distance: Pearson's χ²

$$\chi^2=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{c}\frac{(n_{ij}-\hat n_{ij})^2}{\hat n_{ij}},\qquad \mathrm{df}=(r-1)(c-1),\qquad V=\sqrt{\frac{\chi^2}{n\,(\min(r,c)-1)}}\in[0,1].$$

Each cell compares observed and expected counts; dividing by \(\hat n_{ij}\) puts small and large cells on the same scale (a miss of 10 matters more where 20 were expected than where 2,000 were). Why \((r-1)(c-1)\) degrees of freedom: once the margins are fixed, filling \(r-1\) rows and \(c-1\) columns of the table determines the rest.

p-value (optional part). If \(X\) and \(Y\) were really independent, \(\chi^2\) would follow approximately a chi-square distribution with df degrees of freedom. The p-value is the probability, under independence, of a \(\chi^2\) at least as large as the observed one: \(p=P(\chi^2_{\mathrm{df}}\ge\chi^2_{\mathrm{obs}})=Q\!\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{df}}2,\tfrac{\chi^2_{\mathrm{obs}}}2\right)\), the regularized upper incomplete gamma function, computed here from scratch. A small p says the data would be surprising if the variables were independent.

χ² grows with \(n\): the same proportions observed on ten times more units give ten times the χ². That is why we also report Cramér's \(V\), which does not depend on \(n\), and test it in section 05.

Association is not causation: Berkeley, 1973

Graduate admissions at UC Berkeley, fall 1973 (Bickel, Hammel & O'Connell, Science, 1975). The aggregated table of gender × outcome is strongly dependent: men were admitted more often. Is the admission process biased against women? Condition on the third variable, the department applied to.

Admission rate by department (circle area = applicants)
χ² of gender × admission, overall and within each department

What happened: Simpson's paradox

Within departments, women are admitted at similar rates, and in department A at a clearly higher rate (the only significant stratum). The aggregate dependence comes from a confounder: women applied mostly to departments C–F, which admit few candidates of either gender, while men applied mostly to A and B, which admit most of them. Gender is associated with department, and department with admission. The Mantel–Haenszel test, which pools the six within-department tables, finds no evidence of a gender effect once department is held fixed ().

Lessons that hold for any χ² verdict, including the security ones of this page:

  • Association is symmetric, causation is not. χ² is the same if X and Y are swapped, so it can never say which one drives the other. In NSL-KDD the attack type determines the protocol (a smurf flood is ICMP by construction), not the other way round.
  • Hidden variables. A dependence can be produced or reversed by a third variable, as here. Conditioning on it (stratifying) is the observational remedy.
  • Causal claims need design. Randomized experiments, or explicit causal models with their assumptions, are what turn "related" into "causes". The broken generator of the RNG dataset is the rare case where we know the cause: each output is computed from the previous one.

Conclusions

Every row is recomputed from the raw records when the page loads.

Independent or dependent? The two network variables of NSL-KDD are strongly dependent: the protocol of a connection says a lot about whether, and how, it is an attack (V ≈ 0.23 for protocol × category, larger for the connection flag). The two generators show the use of the test in cryptography: the outputs of a cryptographically secure generator pass (in about 95% of page loads, since they are drawn fresh each time and a 5% test rejects a true independence 5% of the time), consecutive low bits of a power-of-two LCG are perfectly dependent (V = 1), which is why χ² tests are part of randomness test suites. Berkeley shows that a significant χ² is a statement about the table, not about the mechanism behind it.

Reading the numbers. χ² and p answer "could this table come from independent variables?"; with tens of thousands of units even tiny associations become significant, so the size of the association (V, residuals, conditional profiles) has to be read alongside the p-value.

Implementation notes

  • Tables from raw data. Records are scanned once; levels are discovered in order of frequency and counted in a Map. Berkeley's published counts are expanded into 4,526 individual records first, so it goes through the same code path.
  • NSL-KDD. Test set KDDTest+ (Tavallaee et al., 2009), 22,544 connections; the 39 attack labels are grouped into the usual four categories (DoS, Probe, R2L, U2R), with the grouping listed in the source code.
  • p-value. Regularized incomplete gamma by series (x < a+1) or Lentz's continued fraction, in log space so that p-values far below 10−300 are still reported as a power of ten. Checked against SciPy and mpmath.
  • Permutation test. 999 Fisher–Yates shuffles of Y with a seeded PRNG; p = (1 + #{χ²* ≥ χ²}) / 1000.
  • Mantel–Haenszel. 2×2×K test with continuity correction and the MH common odds ratio, as in R's mantelhaen.test.
  • Generators. crypto.getRandomValues versus the LCG \(x_{k+1}=(1103515245\,x_k+12345)\bmod 2^{31}\); X and Y are the two low bits of consecutive outputs.
